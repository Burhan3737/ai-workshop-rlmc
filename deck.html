Two things follow from that.

Because it predictsHow you ask changes everything

Better words in → better prediction out. That's Level 1.

Because it predictsIt can be confidently wrong

Powerful. And never to be trusted blindly.

The lazy prompt — a useless wall of text

The good prompt — same topic, four things added

You are a biology tutor (ROLE). I'm revising for an exam and keep mixing up the light and dark reactions of photosynthesis (CONTEXT). Explain it in 5 simple steps with one everyday analogy, then ask me 3 questions to test myself (TASK). Keep it under 200 words, no jargon (FORMAT).

A good prompt has four parts.

  • RRole — who it should be  "you are a tutor…"
  • CContext — your situation  "I'm revising and I keep mixing up…"
  • TTask — what to actually do  "explain, then quiz me"
  • FFormat — the shape back  "5 steps, under 200 words, no jargon"

Verify01:47

Three ways it will burn you.

Failure 01Outdated information

Its training has a cutoff. Facts, prices and guidelines move. It won't tell you which one you're getting.

Failure 02Invented details

It makes up specific facts, numbers and quotes that sound exactly right.

Failure 03Context mismatch

US and UK guidance is not Pakistani practice. Different availability, different resistance, different protocols.

Verify01:58 · PHOTOGRAPH THIS

Four questions. Every time.

  • 01"Cite it, or tell me you can't."
  • 02"What would change this answer?"
  • 03"What's the strongest argument against what you just told me?"
  • 04"Which fact here is most likely to be out of date?"
The hard rule: never accept a number — a dose, a date, a statistic — without a source you can open. In your field, a confident wrong answer isn't a typo. It's a patient.

05 · Delegate it · Claude Code02:05 · tap to copy

Research it. Then build the presentation.

You are my research assistant and presentation designer (ROLE). I'm a medical student who wants an evidence-based guide to studying smarter for exams (CONTEXT). Research the study techniques proven to work — active recall, spaced repetition, interleaving — from credible sources and cite them, then turn it into a clean slide presentation (TASK). Make it a single self-contained HTML deck: 6–8 slides, one idea each, a sources slide at the end, works offline (FORMAT).
